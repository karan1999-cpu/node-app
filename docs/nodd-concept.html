The idea
Two strangers swap contact details from their phones in seconds. Neither sees the other's phone number. They talk through the app and can share the number later, or cut the connection whenever they want.
Version 1, built
An Android app you can install today. Your QR holds a standard contact card, so anyone who scans it with their phone camera gets Add contact with your name and number filled in, and just taps Save. It needs no server or hosting. The number-free chat and web card move to version 2.
Does it exist?
Fast sharing, yes. Apple has NameDrop (iPhone to iPhone) and Google launched Tap to Share in August 2026 (Android to Android, some phones only). Business-card apps like Blinq, HiHello and Popl also exist. Number-free, take-it-back sharing that works between any two phones, no. That gap is where Nodd fits.
Play Store cost
US$25 one-time (about ₹2,200) for the developer account. If you build it yourselves, the first year costs roughly ₹4,000 to ₹30,000 in cash. Hiring a freelancer takes it to about ₹1.6 to 4.4 lakh.
Time to launch
About 12 weeks, including the 14-day closed test Google requires from new personal developer accounts.
Recommendation
Conditional go In the long run, build it as a privacy app, because Google and Apple now give fast swapping away for free. Version 1 shares your number directly, so you can test it at real events now without hosting. Add the private features once people are using it.

Already solved, for free

Phones exchanging contact cards quickly. NameDrop and Tap to Share are built into the phone, so "faster sharing" alone will not make anyone install another app.

The open gap

Meeting someone you are not ready to trust yet. You want to stay reachable without handing over a number that leads to spam, unwanted calls, or your WhatsApp profile, and you want to be able to undo it later.

Perfect use

The best moment for Nodd is when someone says "give me your number" and you would rather not.

The flow

How it works

Version 1, in the app now

  1. ShowTap "Show my QR". The screen jumps to full brightness so it scans fast.
  2. ScanThey point their phone camera or Google Lens at it. No app needed, iPhone included.
  3. SaveTheir phone opens Add contact with your details filled in. They tap Save.

Version 2, once there is hosting

  1. OpenOne tap on the home-screen widget shows your card's QR code.
  2. ScanThey point their camera at it. With no app, a web card opens. With the app, you connect at once.
  3. ConnectedEach person sees only what the other chose to share. Phone numbers stay hidden.
  4. TalkChat through Nodd. The first message arrives as a request, so nobody can spam you.
  5. Decide laterShare your number, keep it forever, let it expire, or revoke it. Revoking closes the chat for both.

Screens

The UI

Six key screens of the full version. The design rule: the number is always visibly locked, and every action that reveals more is a deliberate tap. Version 1 ships the card and QR screens. The web card, chat and People list come with version 2.

Scope

Features: what's built and what's next

Version 1 Built · in the app

  • Your card: name, mobile, email, work, Instagram, LinkedIn, website. Saved only on your phone.
  • Switch each detail on or off for the QR.
  • The QR holds a standard contact card (vCard). Android cameras, Google Lens and the iPhone camera all offer Add contact.
  • Full screen brightness while the QR shows.
  • Send as contact file. Through WhatsApp, Quick Share or Bluetooth, for people who aren't nearby.
  • Built-in scanner. Opens Add contact with the other person's details filled in.
  • This plan, inside the app.
  • No permission pop-ups. The app never asks for camera or contacts access.

Version 2 Needs hosting

  • A private web link in the QR instead of your number.
  • Web card for people without the app. Save contact or send a message from the browser.
  • Private chat with message requests.
  • Connections expire after 24 hours, 7 days, or never. Revoke any time. The QR refreshes every minute.
  • Sign in with Google and back up your card.
  • Private "met at" notes and a People list.
  • Block, report, and delete account. Required by Google Play once there's chat.
  • Later: Nearby, NFC tap, calls without numbers, event mode, iPhone app, Nodd Plus.

Publishing

Getting onto Google Play

The steps in order, with the rules as they stand in October 2026.

  1. Choose the account type

    Personal: needs your government ID and a 14-day closed test (step 6). Organisation: needs a registered business and a free D-U-N-S number, which can take a few weeks. It skips the closed-test rule and lists the business, not you, as the developer.

  2. Create the Play Console account

    Pay US$25 once (about ₹2,200). Verify your identity, then confirm you have a real Android device through the Play Console app.

  3. Build to Google's current bar

    New apps must target Android 16 (API level 36), which has been required since 31 August 2026. Upload as an Android App Bundle (.aab) with Play App Signing.

  4. Fill in the policy forms

    Privacy policy URL, Data safety form, content rating questionnaire, target audience (set it to 18+), ads declaration, and an account-deletion link that works both in the app and on the web.

  5. Prepare the store listing

    App name up to 30 characters, short description up to 80, full description up to 4,000. A 512×512 icon, a 1024×500 feature graphic, and 2 to 8 phone screenshots.

  6. Run the closed test

    At least 12 testers must stay opted in for 14 days in a row. Recruit 15 to 20 so a few dropping out doesn't restart the clock.

  7. Apply for production access

    Answer Google's questions about the test. The review usually takes a few days and can take longer for new accounts.

  8. Publish with a staged rollout

    Release to 20% of users first, watch for crashes, then go to 100%.

Rules that apply to this app in particular

  • Chat with strangers counts as user-generated content. You need terms of use, in-app reporting, blocking, and someone who acts on reports.
  • Child safety standards for social apps. Publish a child-safety standards page, include an in-app report button, and name a contact person. Setting the audience to 18+ also keeps the app out of the stricter Families rules.
  • Permissions. Ask only for camera (QR), nearby devices, and NFC when those features ship. Save contacts through Android's own "Add contact" screen so you never need the contacts permission. Avoid background location.
  • India: IT Rules 2021. Publish a grievance officer's name and contact in the app and on the website.
  • India: DPDP Act. Most duties on notice, consent, user rights and security apply from 13 May 2027. Build consent and deletion in now so nothing needs rework later.

Naming

The name

Top pick Nodd.

Swap contacts. Keep your number.

The small nod two strangers give each other before swapping details. Four letters, easy to say in English and Hindi, and it works as a verb: "Nodd me." A quick search found no Google Play app with this name in October 2026.

HushcardSays "private" outright. Longer, and less fun to say.

Next step

How to decide

Go on to version 2 if a two-week test shows

  • At least half the people you show the QR to save you without any help.
  • At least 20 of 30 people you ask say they avoid giving their number to strangers.
  • One event organiser (college fest, meetup, co-working space) agrees to pilot it.
  • Someone on the team owns safety: reports, blocking, and the grievance contact.

My recommendation

Worth building as a privacy app. Not worth building as a contact-swap app alone, because Google and Apple already give that away free.

Version 1 needs no hosting, so test it now. Install it on a few phones and take it to two or three real events. Watch whether people scan and save without help, and ask whether they'd rather not hand over their number. If they would, add hosting and build version 2. If they don't care, you have learned that for almost nothing.
